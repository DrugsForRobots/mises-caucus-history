Chapter 1 · Overview

The Story In Brief

Overview · 4 min read · 1 sections

Timeline At A Glance

The Mises Caucus began as a Facebook group in August 2017, started by Michael Heise, a Pennsylvania activist then in his late twenties who had come into politics through Ron Paul's 2008 campaign and spent the next decade in street activism outside the Libertarian Party motherjones.com research notes 02. Its founders said it existed to bring the energy of the Ron Paul movement back into a party they thought had squandered it on the 2016 Johnson–Weld ticket Reason The Scott Horton Show Claim · Heise. Its critics said it was the revival of Murray Rothbard's 1990s paleolibertarian strategy, a turn toward the populist right, and a door left open to bigots research notes 03 SPLC / Hatewatch Claim · Sarwark, SPLC and others.

The caucus spent five years organizing. It lost the national chair race in 2018 and again in 2020, won seats on the national committee, and built a network of podcasts, state chapters and a federal PAC (Mises PAC, registered March 21, 2019) 2018 Convention Minutes, p. 30 docquery.fec.gov. In 2021 the fight in New Hampshire drove the national chair, Joe Bishop-Henchman, to resign, and the caucus openly called its plan a takeover research notes 07. At the Reno convention in May 2022 it won almost everything: Angela McArdle took the chair with 69.55 percent, its slate took the board, the abortion plank was deleted, and the platform's sentence condemning bigotry was replaced research notes 08.

Control proved harder than capture. State parties in New Mexico, Virginia and Massachusetts broke away and formed a rival national body; Michigan split into two parties and the national party went to court research notes 09 research notes 18. The party's sustaining membership, already falling before Reno, kept falling Record, and each side blamed the other research notes 17. In 2024 McArdle brought Donald Trump to speak at the national convention; the delegates then rejected the caucus's presidential candidate, Michael Rectenwald, and nominated Chase Oliver research notes 10. Heise, who had left the caucus chair to run Rectenwald's campaign, endorsed Trump that October while paid by the national party as a contractor research notes 19.

After McArdle resigned in January 2025, Heise ran to replace her and lost to Steven Nekhaila, 9 votes to 6, with board members the caucus had endorsed voting against him Claim · Reason research notes 11. At the 2026 convention the caucus slate lost every officer and at-large seat, and the new board disaffiliated the New Hampshire party, the caucus's first stronghold research notes 12 research notes 18. On October 27, 2026 the caucus, through Mises PAC, launched the American Liberty Party: a published plan to take over, rename and rebrand the Libertarian Party at its 2028 convention and draft Thomas Massie research notes 12.

The good, as its advocates tell it: it brought Ron Paul–era activists, major podcast audiences, and a sharp anti-war, anti-Fed, anti-lockdown message into a party they thought timid; it filled conventions; it won the release of Ross Ulbricht Claim · McArdle and the caucus. The bad, as the record shows: it never delivered the growth it promised by its own measures (members, money, elections); the party it ran shrank and split Record; its founder lost the chair vote to a board it had helped elect. The ugly, as its critics charge: associations with white nationalists, edgy and sometimes cruel posts (mostly from the New Hampshire party rather than the caucus itself), and a willingness to trade the party's independence for access to Trump Claim · SPLC, Sarwark, Bishop-Henchman, the Classical Liberal Caucus and others. The caucus denies racism and calls its accusers partisan.

Michael Heise Before The Caucus

to 2017 · 5 min read · 6 sections

The public record of Michael Heise's life before he founded the Mises Caucus is thin, and most of it comes from Heise himself. The research for this history found no public source giving his birthplace, his schooling, a degree or any military service research notes 01. What survives is a picture of a Ron Paul activist from southeastern Pennsylvania who spent the years after 2008 in street protest and independent media, worked in a law office, and came to the Libertarian Party only in 2016 research notes 01 research notes 02.

Age & Home

Mother Jones, in Tim Murphy's long account The Spectacular Implosion of the Libertarian Party (May+June 2024 issue), describes the founding moment as "a 28-year-old from the Philadelphia suburbs named Michael Heise started a Facebook group called the Libertarian Party Mises Caucus" motherjones.com. If that age is right, he was born about 1989 (Inference, from the Mother Jones figure; no birth date was found anywhere, and no other source gives an age) research notes 01.

Where he lived is consistent across sources but not precise. His Steemit profile, opened in August 2016, gave Lancaster, Pennsylvania (Record, self-entered) peakd.com. On The Scott Horton Show, recorded November 22, 2019 and posted November 25, he said he had "moved out here from Amish country a little over a year ago, and I started attending the city council meetings" The Scott Horton Show, meaning Norristown in Montgomery County Claim · Heise. The Mises PAC's first filing with the Federal Election Commission, in March 2019, lists him at a Norristown mailing address as "Heise | Michael | [PO box omitted] | Norristown | PA | Chair" docquery.fec.gov Record. Where he was born and raised is a gap.

Ron Paul & The Street Years

Every account Heise has given starts in 2008. His own introduction on the Steem/Hive blogging site, dated March 19, 2018, reads: "I am a peace, freedom and decentralization activist and student of crypto with my roots in the 08 Ron Paul Revolution." peakd.com Claim · Heise. In an October 2024 essay, Why I'm Voting for Trump, reposted on Ron Paul Forums, he wrote: "In 2008 and 2012, I wrote in Ron Paul. In 2016, I voted for Gary Johnson" ronpaulforums.com Claim · Heise.

Between 2008 and 2016 he describes about a decade of activism outside any party. To Scott Horton in 2019 he said: "I only entered the, the, the libertarian party in, in 2016 and, uh, prior to that, I was an activist for 10 years." The Scott Horton Show. He added: "I earned my stripes doing cop blocking and, and writing and, and doing man on the street interviews" The Scott Horton Show Claim · Heise.

His 2018 introduction lists the work: "Co-Hosting Adam Vs The Man with Adam Kokesh, starting my own podcast “The Big Plantation”" peakd.com, and "writing for indie news outlets like Filming Cops, doing man in the street videos on my youtube channel" peakd.com. He also says he helped organise "the Nationwide End the Fed event back in 2012 that happened at every federal reserve bank simultaneously nationwide" peakd.com and a Philadelphia End the Fed block party sponsored by the Nexus conference organisers peakd.com Claim · Heise. None of these is dated in his own account, and the Kokesh co-hosting could not be dated at all research notes 02.

One outside voice confirms part of it. Steve Scheetz of the Pennsylvania party, commenting on Independent Political Report (IPR) on October 1, 2017, wrote: "I worked with one of the cofounders of the Misses Caucus. (Michael Heise) to create an End The Fed Rally in Philadelphia." Independent Political Report Claim · Scheetz. Given its Nexus sponsorship, the rally was probably in 2017 Inference research notes 02.

Work

Heise's working life before party politics rests mostly on search-engine excerpts of a LinkedIn profile that could not be opened. They show a job as "Assistant/Paralegal in Training. Sbarbaro Law Offices. Jan 2017 - Jan" (the end date reads January 2021 in the notes) and a job as "Patient Caregiver. TerraVida Holistic Centers. May 2020 - Jun 2021" , TerraVida being a Pennsylvania medical-marijuana dispensary chain Claim · self-published profile research notes 01. The law-office job is backed by his own 2018 words: "When I am not on the warpath for individual liberty, I work in a law office" peakd.com. No business ownership was found, and his entries in federal filings only ever name the caucus or the Mises PAC as employer research notes 01.

The 2016 Primary & The Party

Heise joined the Libertarian Party in 2016 and voted for Gary Johnson that November Claim · Heise The Scott Horton Show ronpaulforums.com. Whom he backed in the 2016 Republican primary is contested. In a February 2018 IPR comment thread, a commenter signing as Andy wrote: "Michael Heise was supporting Rand Paul's run in the Republican primary. Prior to that he was a Ron Paul supporter." independentpoliticalreport.com. Thomas Knapp, in the same thread, half-remembered the founder being "disappointed that the GOP didn't go with his libertarian hero, Ted Cruz" independentpoliticalreport.com; the commenters themselves were unsure. The Rand Paul account fits Heise's Paul lineage; the Cruz version looks like a mix-up Inference. Heise has not settled it in any source found research notes 02.

Nothing found shows Heise holding any role in the 2016 Johnson campaign, in Campaign for Liberty, in Young Americans for Liberty, or in any state party office or candidacy before 2017 research notes 02. His first recorded party role came after the founding: LPedia lists him as one who "was a delegate seated with the delegation of the Libertarian Party of Pennsylvania at the 2018 National Convention" lpedia.org Record.

Why He Says He Founded It

Heise's own explanation is a comparison of two campaigns. On Scott Horton's show in 2019 he said: "it was my comparison between the Ron Paul revolution and the experience of the Gary Johnson campaign" The Scott Horton Show, and that the contrast of "how fun versus how miserable the experience was and that kind of catalyzed the Mises caucus in the first place." The Scott Horton Show Claim · Heise. The caucus's purpose, as he put it, was to "blow on the embers of the Ron Paul revolution and reignited within the libertarian party" The Scott Horton Show.

Reason's reporters tell it the same way. Brian Doherty, in Mises Caucus Takes Control of Libertarian Party (May 29, 2022), wrote: "His disapproval of William Weld, Gary Johnson's running mate in 2016, was an initial inspiration for the caucus' launch." Reason Claim · Doherty, from his interviews with Heise.

The deeper aim was permanence. Mother Jones quotes him on a 2017 podcast: "If we ever did get that back under the guise of an organization as opposed to a single campaign, it never has to die" motherjones.com Claim · Heise. Aaron Harris of the Mises PAC board put it shortly to the Deseret News (Brigham Tomco, October 27, 2022): "In a nutshell it was to bring the Ron Paul Revolution to the Libertarian Party" Deseret News Claim · Harris.

How Critics Describe His Roots

Mother Jones places him on the conspiracy fringe of the Paul movement: "A fan of Alex Jones and Infowars as a teenager, he became active in Paul’s campaign" motherjones.com. It says he led anti-Fed protests holding a bullhorn bearing a sticker that called 9/11 an inside job, and that after Paul left the stage "he also thought the LP was a “joke” and found Johnson uninspiring" motherjones.com Claim · Mother Jones; the sourcing for these details is not shown in the article, and they remain allegations.

The Road To 2017

2008–2017 · 7 min read · 6 sections

The Mises Caucus grew out of two long arguments inside American libertarianism: what to do with the energy of the Ron Paul campaigns, and whether libertarianism should carry a cultural message or stay a narrow political one. The 2016 Gary Johnson and Bill Weld ticket brought the first argument to a head. The summer of 2017, with Jeff Deist's blood and soil speech, the Charlottesville rally and the party chair's tweets against the Mises Institute, brought the second research notes 03.

The Ron Paul Movement & The 2008 Split

Ron Paul's 2007–08 Republican campaign drew a new mass of activists to libertarian ideas, though not all libertarians welcomed it Claim · Brian Doherty, Reason, July 27, 2007 Reason. The Libertarian National Committee (LNC) saw an opening: in December 2007 it adopted a resolution that "the Libertarian National Committee urges Congressman Ron Paul to seek the presidential nomination of the Libertarian Party" Reason (Record, as reported by Reason's David Weigel on January 7, 2009; the minutes themselves were not re-read).

The movement's darker inheritance surfaced in January 2008, when Reason's Julian Sanchez and David Weigel reported that movement veterans and a campaign source named Lew Rockwell as the main ghostwriter of Paul's 1990s newsletters, with their racial content reason.com Claim · Sanchez and Weigel's sources. "Rockwell has denied responsibility for the newsletters' contents" reason.com. The question is unresolved; both accounts stand.

Paul lost the nomination, and the Libertarian Party chose the former Republican congressman Bob Barr in May 2008 research notes 03. On September 10, 2008, Barr skipped Ron Paul's National Press Club event for third-party candidates and held his own press conference two hours later, telling reporters "I'm not interested in third parties getting the most possible votes" en.wikinews.org (Record of the event, per Wikinews, September 12, 2008). Paul went on to endorse the Constitution Party's Chuck Baldwin Reason, and Barr's running mate Wayne Allyn Root later named the party's problem as "the ghost of Ron Paul" Reason Claim · Root, Reason, David Weigel, November 24, 2008. This is the split Heise's generation of activists inherited: a Paul movement that had grown outside the party and a party that had failed to bring it in Inference.

Thick Versus Thin

Reason's editors had declared a Libertarian Moment in November 2008, and the idea peaked with a New York Times Magazine cover story in August 2014 Claim · Gillespie and Welch; the Times article was not obtained reason.com research notes 03. That same year the culture fight opened. On March 12, 2014, Jeffrey Tucker published Against Libertarian Brutalism at the Foundation for Economic Education, arguing that "libertarians can generally be divided into two camps: humanitarians and brutalists." fee.org and closing: "Will libertarianism be brutalist or humanitarian? Everyone needs to decide." fee.org (Record of publication). This is the opening of what libertarians came to call the thick versus thin debate: whether a libertarian must also hold broader humane or egalitarian values (thick), or need only oppose aggression by the state (thin) research notes 03.

One of Tucker's loudest opponents was Christopher Cantwell. A Ron Paul 2012 supporter who had moved to New Hampshire, he called himself a brutalist and, as David Weigel later reported in the Washington Post (August 24, 2017), answered that "egalitarianism is not the means or end of libertarianism, and saying otherwise in hopes of attracting Democrats into our ranks is illusory" washingtonpost.com Claim · Cantwell. The Free State Project had already expelled him for promoting violence, probably in 2013 (Record of the board's action as quoted by George Donnelly; the year is an Inference) morelibertynow.com. Tom Woods hosted Cantwell once on his podcast in 2014; Woods later wrote that "Cantwell appears to have shifted toward white nationalism around 2017." The Tom Woods Show Claim · Woods. That 2014 episode became ammunition for critics after Charlottesville research notes 03.

2016: Johnson, Weld & The Orlando Boos

The 2016 primary season showed the gap between the party's nominee and its radical wing. On the Fox Business Stossel forum, aired April 1, 2016, Gary Johnson was asked whether a Jewish baker must bake a cake for a Nazi wedding and answered "that would be my contention, yes." independentpoliticalreport.com (Record of the broadcast, as reported by William Saturn on IPR, April 2, 2016).

At the Orlando convention debate on May 28, 2016, ABC News reported: "Johnson was the only candidate who said he would require drivers to have licenses, citing possible dangers -- such as blind drivers. The crowd responded by booing loudly." abcnews.com, and "Gary Johnson also received boos for saying he would have signed the Civil Rights Act of 1964." abcnews.com (Record of the event, per Ryan Struyk and Ines de la Cuetara, May 29, 2016).

The convention minutes record the outcome. Johnson won on the second ballot, "Gary Johnson 518 55.819%" 2016 Convention Minutes, p. 27. For vice president, Weld beat Larry Sharpe narrowly on the second ballot: "William Weld 441 50.573%" 2016 Convention Minutes, p. 33 to "Larry Sharpe 409 46.904%" 2016 Convention Minutes, p. 33 Record. Sharpe, the runner-up, later spoke for the Mises Caucus's chair candidate in 2018; he was a caucus ally, not a critic 2018 Convention Minutes, p. 28.

On November 1, 2016, Weld went on MSNBC and said "I'm here vouching for Mrs. Clinton, and I think it's high time somebody did" cnn.com and "Gary and I have not agreed on a number of substantive issues in this campaign" cnn.com (Record of the broadcast, as reported by Eli Watkins of CNN). For Heise and those who would join him, this was the moment the ticket stopped being theirs (Inference, from Heise's and Reason's accounts of Weld as an inspiration for the caucus) Reason.

The Mises Institute's Verdict

Writers in the Mises Institute orbit gave their verdict on LewRockwell.com within days of the election. Jeff Deist, the Institute's president, wrote on November 9, 2016 of "the milquetoast version being peddled very unsuccessfully by Gary Johnson and Bill Weld" lewrockwell.com, and argued that "the overwhelming threat to liberty today is from the Left, not the Right." lewrockwell.com Claim · Deist. Their complaint was the one Heise would later make: the ticket had won votes but not converts Inference.

Summer 2017: Blood & Soil, Charlottesville & The Chair's Tweets

On July 28, 2017, Deist gave a Mises University talk published as For a New Libertarian. He argued that "it is reasonable to believe that a more libertarian society would be less libertine and more culturally conservative" mises.org, and closed: "blood and soil and God and nation still matter to people. Libertarians ignore this at the risk of irrelevance." mises.org (Record of publication). Steve Horwitz answered on Bleeding Heart Libertarians on August 4: "The paleo-libertarian seed that Ron Paul, Murray Rothbard, and Lew Rockwell planted in the 1990s has come to bear some really ugly fruit" bleedingheartlibertarians.com Claim · Horwitz.

The Unite the Right rally in Charlottesville followed on August 11 and 12. Cantwell became one of its best-known faces, and the Washington Post reported that "in Cantwell's own words, he had come to racism and anti-Semitism through libertarianism" washingtonpost.com Claim · Cantwell, via Weigel.

On August 14, Sarwark turned on Tom Woods over Woods's defence of Rothbard's paleo strategy, tweeting: "TFW all you learned from Murray Rothbard was his worst political strategy ever." Reason (Record of the tweet as embedded by Matt Welch in Reason, July 4, 2018). Reason reports Woods replied by calling Sarwark a pansy with a very low IQ, and that Sarwark then called the Mises Institute "the preferred choice of actual Nazis." motherjones.com Claim · Reason's and Mother Jones's account of the replies; the tweet's ID dates it to 20:45 UTC that day reason.com. The next day Vice Chair Arvin Vohra, in a post Sarwark shared, wrote: "Mises Institute has been turned into a sales funnel for the White Nationalist branch of the Alt Right." independentpoliticalreport.com Claim · Vohra, IPR, Jordan Willow Evans, August 15, 2017. Executive Director Wes Benedict's release the same day said: "There is no room for racists and bigots in the Libertarian Party." Reason (Record of the release, as quoted).

The SPLC adds two items. Sarwark signed a post-Charlottesville open letter against fascism in the movement, which Woods rejected Claim · SPLC; the letter itself was not retrieved SPLC / Hatewatch. And "Three days after the deadly August 2017 white supremacist rally in Charlottesville, Virginia, Smith hosted neo-Nazi Christopher Cantwell." SPLC / Hatewatch, with Dave Smith saying he was "sympathetic to the alt-right to a large degree" SPLC / Hatewatch while disagreeing with its tactics Claim · SPLC; the episode itself was not checked. Which came first, the open letter or the tweet at Woods, is not established; Reason puts the tweet first and the SPLC the letter research notes 03.

Two Accounts Of Why The Caucus Was Born

Supporters and critics tell the origin differently. Supporters say the Johnson and Weld boom left nothing behind. Heise told Reason in 2024 that Johnson voters left "no lasting movement, no return on investment on those votes" Reason Claim · Heise. Scott Horton described the caucus generation as "These are the kids who came up in 2008 and 2012 inspired by Ron Paul" Reason, and Dave Smith said "The priorities of the Mises Caucus have always been basically the priorities of the Ron Paul Revolution" Reason (Claims: Horton; Smith; Reason, June 15, 2022).

Critics tie the caucus to the old paleo strategy and to Charlottesville. Brianna Coyle, quitting at Reno in 2022, said: "This is the paleo strategy happening yet again." Reason. Sarwark said in 2022 that the caucus wanted "to change fundamental aspects that are definitional to Libertarian philosophy, to suit their preferences." SPLC / Hatewatch (Claims: Coyle; Sarwark).

Both accounts can be true in sequence. Reason's reporting has Heise's motive forming after 2016, while the Sarwark–Woods fight gave the new group a cause and an enemy in August 2017 (Inference, from LP-311 and LP-252) Reason Reason. Sarwark himself said after the 2018 convention that "The people who were mad about the Charlottesville and near-Charlottesville comments, that was the core of the support" Reason for the caucus's chair candidate Claim · Sarwark.

Founding, 2017

The Mises Caucus began as a Facebook group in mid-August 2017, most likely on August 14, the same day the party chair called the Mises Institute the preferred choice of Nazis. It had a website and a written platform by the turn of the year, but no political action committee until March 2019 research notes 04.

The Date Question

No source gives the founding date from a primary record. Mother Jones writes: "That same day, a 28-year-old from the Philadelphia suburbs named Michael Heise started a Facebook group called the Libertarian Party Mises Caucus." motherjones.com, meaning the day of Sarwark's tweet, and adds: "By the next morning the group had 600 members." motherjones.com Claim · Mother Jones; the article does not give its source. The tweet's ID fixes that day as August 14 Inference reason.com. According to Mother Jones, the group's first post read "“We’re bringing libertarianism back,” it announced over a meme of Paul walking away from an explosion." motherjones.com.

Other sources fit, roughly. John M. Hudak, an administrator of the anti-alt-right Facebook group Fakertarians who later moderated the caucus group, wrote: "I initially joined the Libertarian Party Mises Caucus Facebook group sometime around August 2017, within weeks of its creation." fakertarians.wordpress.com Claim · Hudak, undated post written after November 2019. An archived copy of the caucus's Twitter profile shows the account was created on August 23, 2017 Record web.archive.org. The World Socialist Web Site, a hostile outlet, wrote on March 3, 2023 that the caucus "was founded by Michael Heise on August 20, 2017." wsws.org Claim · WSWS. Steven Clyde's caucus podcast of January 18, 2019 began: "I chatted with Michael Heise about the Libertarian Party Mises Caucus which he founded in late-2017." lpmisescaucus.com, and the caucus's July 2018 report said the caucus "did not exist a year ago" web.archive.org (Claims: caucus).

The Facebook group's own creation date would settle it, but no archived copy of the group page exists research notes 04. Dave Smith later recalled the start for Heise in 2023: "this started with you just like posting on Facebook like, hey, you know what?" Part of the Problem / transcript…, adding "it gets like 13 likes or something like that." Part of the Problem / transcript… Claim · Smith. That modest picture sits awkwardly beside Mother Jones's 600 overnight members; both are reported here.

LPedia says: "The Mises Caucus was founded in 2017 by Michael Heise and fellow Libertarians." lpedia.org, and Scheetz called Heise one of its cofounders in 2017 Independent Political Report. Who the other founders were is a gap research notes 02.

The First Months

In September 2017 Heise met Ron Paul and Judge Andrew Napolitano at the Nexus conference. He wrote in February 2018: "I had the pleasure of discussing the caucus and our goals with both Dr. Paul and the judge at the Nexus Conference in September" web.archive.org Claim · Heise. The First Platform

By January 5, 2018, the website carried a Statement of Purpose and seven planks: Property Rights, Self-Determination, Economics, Money, Decorum, Lifestyle Choices and Identity Politics Record web.archive.org. It opened: "Statement of Purpose: To promote Austrian economics within the Libertarian Party, to stress the importance of sound economics as critical to the Libertarian Party message" web.archive.org. The planks were radical on the state: "the Mises Caucus favors radical decentralization of, and secession from, all government and political units." web.archive.org. On culture they were thin: "The Mises Caucus takes no stance on personal, cultural, and social preferences." web.archive.org. And on identity politics they were blunt: "The Mises Caucus categorically rejects all forms of identity politics as nothing more than weaponized tribal collectivism that is antithetical to individualism." web.archive.org.

The Decorum plank praised the caucus's namesake: "Ludwig von Mises was a brilliant moral philosopher and an accomplished economist, but above all, he was a gentleman." web.archive.org. On May 1, 2018 the caucus revised the platform. It added a War plank, saying "after a conversation with Scott Horton, we have come to recognize that not only is war important, it’s our comparative advantage in politics" web.archive.org, and explained that "we removed the “Decorum” plank as decorum isn’t a policy position but a code of conduct" web.archive.org Record. In August 2018 a post titled Plank 6 – An Exposition explained the caucus's thin stance: "The goal, then, is not to keep thick libertarians out; but rather, to ask them to leave their “thick” issues at the door" web.archive.org Record.

The caucus is named for Ludwig von Mises, but its platform and its leading voices draw more on Murray Rothbard, on the early-1990s paleo strategy of Rothbard and Lew Rockwell, and on Hans-Hermann Hoppe. The Books Behind The Name

Mises's Liberalism (1927, Ralph Raico's translation) supplied the line the caucus quoted in its first platform: "If it were in any way possible to grant this right of self-determination to every individual person, it would have to be done." oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo… Mises at once limits it to territorial units for practical reasons Record oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo…. On migration he writes: "There cannot be the slightest doubt that migration barriers diminish the productivity of human labor." oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo… He grants the fear of newcomers, "It cannot be denied that these fears are justified." oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo…, but answers it with liberal government, not border control: "Only the adoption of the liberal program could make the problem of immigration, which today seems insoluble, completely disappear." oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo… He is flat on anarchism: "Liberalism is not anarchism, nor has it anything whatsoever to do with anarchism." oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo… His often-quoted remark on Italian Fascism sits in a chapter that condemns its faith in violence and ends: "Fascism was an emergency makeshift." oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo…

The strategy came from Rothbard's January 1992 essay Right-Wing Populism: A Strategy for the Paleo Movement, saved from LewRockwell.com's 2017 reprint, which notes "This was written in January 1992." lewrockwell.com Its aim was "to tap the masses directly, to short-circuit the dominant media and intellectual elites" lewrockwell.com. Its program included "Abolish the Fed; Attack the Banksters." lewrockwell.com, "Cops must be unleashed, and allowed to administer instant punishment" lewrockwell.com, and "America First. A key point, and not meant to be seventh in priority." lewrockwell.com The historian David Bebnowski (2024) reads Rothbard as saying a paleo coalition "would be possible if a presidential candidate were able to short-circuit the traditional media" jaaas.eu, and sees Trump's 2016 run as that plan realized Claim · Bebnowski; his article does not mention the caucus jaaas.eu.

Three Platforms

Wayback captures of lpmisescaucus.com show three platforms. The first, captured January 5, 2018, set the purpose: "To promote Austrian economics within the Libertarian Party, to stress the importance of sound economics as critical to the Libertarian Party message" web.archive.org. Its seven planks Record included self-determination, concluding "the Mises Caucus favors radical decentralization of, and secession from, all government and political units." web.archive.org; money: "Accordingly, we advocate abolishing the Federal Reserve System." web.archive.org; a decorum plank: "We intend to conduct ourselves in a way that honors his tradition, while exhibiting the utmost decorum in the battle of hearts and minds." web.archive.org; and identity politics: "The Mises Caucus categorically rejects all forms of identity politics as nothing more than weaponized tribal collectivism that is antithetical to individualism." web.archive.org It had no foreign-policy plank Record web.archive.org.

On May 1, 2018, after talks with the antiwar broadcaster Scott Horton, an admin post added war: "not only is war important, it's our comparative advantage in politics, and thus it needs to be a priority." web.archive.org The same post dropped the decorum plank as a code of conduct, not a policy Record web.archive.org. The second platform (captured August 23, 2018, unchanged to June 2022) added: "We promote opposition to war, advocacy of decentralization, and privatization both politically and monetarily as the highest priority issues for all libertarians." web.archive.org Its war plank: "We advocate the abolition of empire including ending the terror war, bringing all the troops home, and closing at least all foreign bases." web.archive.org Decentralization ran "all the way down to the individual" web.archive.org, and on lifestyle: "We take no stance on the personal, cultural, or social preferences of individuals or groups." web.archive.org Secession to the individual and market governance is Rothbard's anarcho-capitalism, not Mises's limited state (Inference: helper 13) oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazo…. Three accounts get blurred in nearly every account of the caucus's messaging, and keeping them apart changes the story (Inference: helper 20) research notes 20:

  1. @LPNH, the New Hampshire affiliate, run from spring 2021 by a team that included Jeremy Kauffman. Most of the notorious posts came from here.
  2. @LPNational, the national account, run from June 2022 by LNC member Dave Benner with Dave Smith advising.
  3. The caucus's own accounts, @LPMisesCaucus and state caucus accounts such as Massachusetts's.

By 2023 LPNH was openly at odds with the national caucus, so its later posts are not caucus messaging in the strict sense, though critics kept counting them that way (Inference: helper 20) redpilldiaries.substack.com. Yet the caucus-led national account deliberately sought an irreverent tone and at least once joined an LPNH post Record LNC-MINUTES 2022-07-30-31 FINAL, p. 136 newrepublic.com.

The Strategy

The caucus built its audience through podcasts: Woods, Smith's Part of the Problem, Scott Horton, and its own Decentralized Revolution feed hosted by Aaron Harris, where in February 2021 Heise, Smith and Harlos "got together for a livestream on the LMPC social media pages on Friday, Feb. 12" LP Mises Caucus. In December 2022 Heise announced "the Mises Caucus' effort to recruit and train 100+ candidates for local races in 2023" lpmisescaucus.com; episodes covered "our plans to avoid cancelation online" lpmisescaucus.com.

Before Reno the caucus strategy document aimed to "make the LP appealing to the wider liberty movement that is largely not currently here with us" Reason. Heise named the targets after winning: "Bitcoiners, people pissed at schools, the major podcast audiences, etc." Reason McArdle's platform: "Social media is usually the first thing someone sees when they interact with us as an organization." Reason And: "Is bold messaging shit posting? No. Bold messaging is courageous, and it tells the truth." Reason Benner reported in July 2022: "we have made a conscious effort to message in a more radical way, with a more irreverent tone" LNC-MINUTES 2022-07-30-31 FINAL, p. 136, and "Dave Smith and I are both aligned on making radical anti-Fed and anti-war messaging our bread and butter" LNC-MINUTES 2022-07-30-31 FINAL, p. 136. In 2023 the LNC's social media committee listed "Dave Smith, Political Commentator, serving in a creative advisory role" LNC-Meeting 2023-03-11-12 FINAL, p. 106.

Michael Heise, 2017–2026

Theme · 5 min read · 4 sections

Founder-Chair, 2017–2023Handing Over The CaucusContractor, Trump Endorser, Chair Candidate, 2024–2025The America First Turn, 2025–2026

Michael Heise founded and ran the caucus and Mises PAC from 2017 to late 2023, steered it to the 2022 sweep, then worked for a presidential campaign, for the party as a paid contractor, and for his own media venture, and in 2026 set out to rebrand the Libertarian Party as a vehicle for the America First movement (Record and Claim) linkedin.com Independent Political Report. His method stayed the same throughout; his goal changed Inference. His earlier life and founding activism are in the chronology chapters.

Founder-Chair, 2017–2023

His LinkedIn listing reads: "Chair and Founder. Mises Political Action Committee and Mises Caucus. Sep 2017 - Oct 2023 6 years 2 months." linkedin.com (Claim; search excerpt only). He worked inside party structures early: in April 2018 the LNC voted to "appoint Michael Heise as the seventh member of the Blockchain" 2018 04 21-22 LNC Minutes-approved, p. 14 Committee, and at New Orleans "Michael Heise (PA) nominated Joshua Smith." 2018 Convention Minutes, p. 27 Record. Mother Jones says Patrick Byrne "gave $5,000 in seed money to the Mises Caucus" motherjones.com (Claim, resting on Heise's own posts as reported). In 2021 the LNC's New Hampshire investigators recorded his denial of the Bannon rumor: "In our interview with Michael Heise, he rejected this" LNC-Meeting 2021-09-04-05 FINAL, p. 132. As PAC chair he was also its largest payee (chapter 17) api.open.fec.gov.

Reno made him the faction's acknowledged strategist; Doherty called him the "behind-the-scenes mastermind" Reason. He told Reason: "The foundation of our strategy is nullification and decentralization" Reason. Reason later paraphrased his view: "The primary goal of the new Libertarian Party isn't winning national elections, which Heise considers delusional" Reason, but spoiler leverage. In July 2022 Horton began his LNC speech: "I want to apologize to Michael Heise and Angela McArdle" The Scott Horton Show.
